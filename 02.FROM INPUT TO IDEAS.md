# FROM INPUT TO IDEAS

### How to Turn What You Hear and Read into Ideas for Speaking

---

## 01 · The Core Principle

Many learners think that speaking means:

> **Question → Think of an idea → Speak**

But this is often unrealistic, especially when the learner has limited language resources or limited knowledge of the topic.

Effective speaking development follows a longer process:

**INPUT → NOTICE → UNDERSTAND → EXTRACT → CONNECT → DEVELOP → EXPRESS → TRANSFER**

The purpose of this process is to help you move from **receiving language** to **generating your own ideas**.

You are not expected to create ideas from nothing.

You learn to **build ideas from input** and gradually make those ideas your own.

---

# 02 · What Counts as Input?

Input is not simply “something you listen to”.

Useful input can include:

* a video
* a podcast
* an interview
* an article
* a conversation
* a lecture
* a personal story
* a news report
* an authentic social-media discussion

Good speaking input should provide at least some of the following:

**Language + Information + Perspectives + Examples + Reasons + Experiences**

This gives you material to think with.

---

# 03 · The Input → Idea Connection

When you listen to or read something, do not try to remember everything.

Instead, extract **idea units**.

For example:

### INPUT

> Social media allows young people to maintain relationships with friends who live far away.

Do not only learn:

**maintain relationships**

Also ask:

* What is the main idea?
* Why does this matter?
* Who benefits?
* What is a possible example?
* Is there a negative side?
* Do I agree?
* Have I experienced something similar?
* Can I connect this to another issue?

Now one sentence has become an **idea network**.

### ONE INPUT IDEA

**Social media helps young people maintain distant relationships.**

↓

**WHY?**
It makes communication easier.

↓

**EXAMPLE?**
Friends can stay in contact after moving to another city.

↓

**RESULT?**
Relationships may be easier to maintain.

↓

**LIMITATION?**
Online communication may not replace face-to-face interaction.

↓

**PERSONAL RESPONSE?**
I think it is useful when combined with real-life interaction.

One sentence has now produced several possible speaking directions.

---

# 04 · The Five-Step Idea Extraction Method

Whenever you encounter useful input, use:

### **1. WHAT?**

What is the speaker/writer saying?

### **2. WHY?**

Why is this important or why does it happen?

### **3. HOW?**

How does it work or happen?

### **4. EXAMPLE?**

What example illustrates the idea?

### **5. SO WHAT?**

What are the consequences, implications, problems or wider connections?

This transforms passive comprehension into active thinking.

---

# 05 · From One Idea to an Idea Network

A strong speaker rarely has only one idea.

Instead, one central idea can generate several branches:

```text
                         CENTRAL IDEA
                              │
          ┌───────────────────┼───────────────────┐
          ↓                   ↓                   ↓
        REASON              EXAMPLE          CONSEQUENCE
          │                   │                   │
          ↓                   ↓                   ↓
       RESULT             EXPERIENCE          PROBLEM
                              │
                              ↓
                         ALTERNATIVE
                              │
                              ↓
                           OPINION
```

For example:

### Topic: Online education

**Central idea:**
Online education provides greater flexibility.

**Reason:**
Students can study at different times.

**Example:**
A student with a full-time job can study in the evening.

**Benefit:**
Education becomes more accessible.

**Problem:**
Students may have less direct interaction.

**Alternative:**
A blended model may combine flexibility with face-to-face learning.

**Opinion:**
For many learners, blended learning may be more effective.

Now the learner has enough material to speak.

# 06 · The Idea Ladder

When you cannot think of what to say, do not ask:

> “What should I say?”

Ask smaller questions.

### THE IDEA LADDER

**TOPIC**
↓
**What is it?**
↓
**Why does it matter?**
↓
**What causes it?**
↓
**What are its benefits?**
↓
**What are its problems?**
↓
**Who is affected?**
↓
**Can I give an example?**
↓
**What might happen as a result?**
↓
**Is there another perspective?**
↓
**What do I think?**

You do not need to answer every question.

Usually, **3–5 strong answers are enough for a short speaking task.**

---

# 07 · Input Becomes a Personal Resource

The goal is not to memorise the original speaker's ideas.

Instead, move through these four stages:

### STAGE 1 · RECEIVE

> “I heard this idea.”

### STAGE 2 · UNDERSTAND

> “I understand why this idea makes sense.”

### STAGE 3 · CONNECT

> “I can connect this idea to an example, experience or another issue.”

### STAGE 4 · OWN

> “I can express this idea in my own words and develop it further.”

This is the transition from **input knowledge** to **usable speaking knowledge**.

---

# 08 · The Language Layer

Ideas alone are not enough.

You also need language to express them.

From the input, collect useful:

* vocabulary
* collocations
* sentence patterns
* discourse markers
* grammatical structures
* opinion phrases
* cause-and-effect expressions
* comparison structures

For example:

### INPUT

> Online education can make learning more accessible.

### LANGUAGE

**make something more accessible**

### IDEA

Accessibility can increase educational opportunities.

### PERSONAL EXPRESSION

> I think online education can make learning more accessible, particularly for people who cannot attend traditional classes.

The learner has moved from:

**Language → Idea → Personal Expression**

---

# 09 · The Three-Layer Speaking Model

Every speaking response can be developed through three layers:

### LAYER 1 · CONTENT

**What do I want to say?**

Idea · Reason · Example · Result

↓

### LAYER 2 · LANGUAGE

**How can I say it?**

Vocabulary · Collocations · Grammar · Chunks

↓

### LAYER 3 · DELIVERY

**How can I deliver it effectively?**

Pronunciation · Stress · Intonation · Fluency · Pausing

A strong speaking lesson should work with **all three layers**.

---

# 10 · Supported Speaking

If you cannot speak independently yet, do not immediately try to speak without support.

Use a support ladder.

### LEVEL 1 · FULL SUPPORT

Question + prompts + keywords + sentence starters

### LEVEL 2 · PARTIAL SUPPORT

Question + keywords

### LEVEL 3 · MINIMAL SUPPORT

Question + topic

### LEVEL 4 · NO SUPPORT

Topic only

The teacher gradually removes support.

### **Support ↓ → Independence ↑**

This is essential.

The purpose of support is not to make speaking easy forever.

Its purpose is to make independence possible.

---

# 11 · From Input to Independent Speaking

Use this progression:

### ROUND 1 · RETELL

What did the speaker say?

### ROUND 2 · RESTRUCTURE

Can you explain the same idea in your own words?

### ROUND 3 · DEVELOP

Can you add a reason, example or consequence?

### ROUND 4 · RESPOND

Do you agree? Why or why not?

### ROUND 5 · EXTEND

Can you introduce another perspective?

### ROUND 6 · TRANSFER

Can you use the same thinking pattern for a new question?

This is where genuine speaking development happens.

---

# 12 · A Complete Practice Cycle

Use the following cycle with a short authentic video or article.

### STEP 1 · LISTEN / READ

Understand the general message.

### STEP 2 · NOTICE

Identify useful language and important ideas.

### STEP 3 · EXTRACT

Write down **3–5 key ideas**.

### STEP 4 · DEVELOP

For each idea, ask:

**Why? · How? · Example? · Result? · Problem?**

### STEP 5 · CONNECT

Connect the ideas to:

**your experience · your knowledge · another issue · your opinion**

### STEP 6 · REPRODUCE

Retell the original content without reading the text.

### STEP 7 · RESPOND

Give your own view using the ideas you developed.

### STEP 8 · TRANSFER

Answer a new question related to the same topic.

### STEP 9 · RECORD

Record yourself for **60–90 seconds**.

### STEP 10 · REVIEW

Identify only **2–3 areas** to improve.

### STEP 11 · REPEAT

Record the response again.

---

# 13 · The Teacher's Role

A skilled teacher is not simply an error corrector.

The teacher helps the learner move through:

**DEPENDENCE → GUIDED PERFORMANCE → INDEPENDENCE**

The teacher may:

* select appropriate input
* model language
* highlight important ideas
* ask development questions
* provide prompts
* supply missing vocabulary
* challenge weak reasoning
* diagnose speaking problems
* give focused feedback
* gradually remove support
* introduce new contexts for transfer

The teacher should ask:

> **“What is preventing this learner from expressing the idea?”**

Is the problem:

**Ideas? · Language? · Organisation? · Pronunciation? · Fluency? · Confidence?**

The answer determines the intervention.

---

# 14 · When You Have No Ideas

Do not panic.

Use the **IDEA RESCUE FRAMEWORK**:

### A · DEFINE

What exactly is the issue?

### B · REASON

Why does it happen?

### C · EXAMPLE

Can I imagine or remember a specific example?

### D · EFFECT

What happens because of it?

### E · CONTRAST

What is the opposite view?

### F · EXPERIENCE

Have I seen or experienced something similar?

### G · OPINION

What do I personally think?

Even if you begin with very little knowledge, these questions can help you construct a response.

---

# 15 · The Final Challenge

After working with the input, close the article or video.

You should now receive:

> **A new question related to the same topic.**

You have:

**30–60 seconds to prepare**

Then:

**60–120 seconds to speak**

Rules:

* No script
* No memorised answer
* Limited notes
* Use at least **2–3 useful expressions** from the input
* Develop at least **2 connected ideas**
* Include at least **one example or explanation**
* Give your own position when appropriate

This is the point where **input becomes transferable speaking ability**.

## The Core Principle

> **Do not expect learners to produce ideas from nothing.**
>
> **Give them meaningful input, teach them how to notice and develop ideas, provide temporary support, and gradually remove that support.**

The ultimate goal is not to reproduce the input.

It is to use the input as a **springboard for independent thought and authentic communication**.

### **INPUT → THINKING → LANGUAGE → EXPRESSION → TRANSFER**

That is the bridge from **learning English** to **using English**.
